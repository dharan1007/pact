TRUST MODEL

Trust without blind access

PACT does not ask you to trust an agent's judgment after giving it broad permissions. It constrains what can happen before authority exists and verifies what happened afterward.

01

Exact-plan authorization

Approval binds to a SHA-256 digest of the precise effects, constraints and canonical base version. Any substitution invalidates authority.

PREVENTS: plan swapping after approval
02

Short-lived capability lease

Commit authority exists only for the approved transaction and expires automatically.

PREVENTS: durable ambient privilege
03

Stale-state rejection

If canonical state changes after preview, the approved transaction cannot commit against the new world.

PREVENTS: acting on obsolete assumptions
04

Negative invariants

PACT verifies what must not change—billing and deletion in the reference transaction—not only intended positive effects.

PREVENTS: collateral side effects
05

Fail-closed persistence

If durable storage fails after an in-memory operation, PACT restores the pre-operation snapshot rather than leaving ghost state.

PREVENTS: memory/durable divergence
06

Tamper-evident receipt chain

Receipts and audit entries are independently hashed and linked so post-hoc mutation is detected.

PREVENTS: unverifiable success claims
07

Detached durable replay index

Provider idempotency evidence is stored as immutable replay records outside the hot canonical CAS object. Replay guarantees remain durable while high-volume resources avoid an ever-growing state record and the contention that comes with rewriting the full replay history on every transaction.

PREVENTS: replay-loss shortcuts and unbounded hot-record growth
08

One approval across a durable cross-resource saga

PACT can freeze an ordered multi-resource plan, bind one human approval to its exact plan hash, execute each provider step with stable idempotency keys, compensate only where a handler explicitly supports a safe inverse, and stop on uncertain outcomes until reconciliation proves what actually happened. Terminal outcomes distinguish committed, compensated and partially committed workflows instead of claiming distributed ACID that providers do not offer.

PREVENTS: unapproved cross-system drift, blind rollback and false all-or-nothing claims
09

Aggregate saga receipt

A terminal cross-resource workflow produces a hash-bound receipt containing the approved plan hash, human and agent approval claims, execution authorization, every durable step outcome, compensation evidence and the truthful final saga state.

PREVENTS: flattening a multi-provider workflow into an unverifiable success flag
10

Negotiated provider guarantees

The production provider registry declares each handler's exact resource and atomic domain plus its conditional-write, idempotency, mutation, reconciliation, compensation and reversibility guarantees. Saga preview rejects a step before approval when its requested guarantees exceed the registered provider contract, and server-only credential references are resolved without exposing token values in public registry metadata.

PREVENTS: approving a workflow whose provider cannot actually deliver the promised safety semantics
